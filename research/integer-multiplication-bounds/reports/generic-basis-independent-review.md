## Finite constructive existence, including indefinite metrics

[The independently authored theorem](review-generic-metric-basis.md)
uses one rational isometry of the nondegenerate ambient tensor metric.
For a kernel basis U and Gram dual V, every deficient front/back square
flag changes by a rank-one reflection update. A chosen nonzero left null
functional and right null vector give two proper linear factors in the
reflection direction. Their nonvanishing places the update vector outside
the old column space and its covector outside the old row space, raising
rank by exactly one.

For a full flag, its determinant after the update has the quadratic
factor `q(w)-2v(w)^T A^-1u(w)`. This polynomial cannot vanish identically:
a nonisotropic rational vector in the kernel's nonzero nondegenerate
orthogonal complement fixes its basis and dual, making this factor q(w).
The properness witness need not be the same for different factors.
Multiplying all factors and q(w) is legitimate in the rational polynomial
integral domain. For F spaces the total degree is at most `2+4F`.
An integer tensor grid with that many plus one values per coordinate has
a nonzero evaluation, by repeated univariate interpolation. A finite
algorithm therefore chooses a direction at each round. Every deficient
flag improves while full flags stay full, so at most the largest kernel
dimension rounds suffice. Metric isometry preserves nondegeneracy at
all subsequent rounds. Indefiniteness creates no exception to this proof.

The finite family is large, with at most `v+v^2+2v^3` members, but h is a
fixed constant of the asymptotic algorithm. No numerical claim that the
full h51 matrix has been produced is made. Giant basis coefficients,
factor tables and a common admissible prime are finite deterministic setup
with their own possibly enormous eventual constants.
