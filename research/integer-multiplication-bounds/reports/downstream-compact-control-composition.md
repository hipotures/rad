## Scoped ceiling and current optimum

Let G_* be the minimum seven-margin saving for any admitted parameters.
Since lambda'>tau, `G_*<=epsilon*a`. Prefix and movement together give

```
G_* <= a*(1-epsilon)/(1+a).
```

Indeed `G_*<=epsilon*a*c` implies `c>=G_*/(epsilon*a)`, while
`G_*<=1-epsilon*(1+c)` implies
`G_*(1+1/a)<=1-epsilon`. Combining the two affine epsilon bounds yields
`G_*<=a/(2+a)`.

Separately, leaf and guard imply
`G_*<epsilon*b*x<b*(1-epsilon)/4`. Combining this with
`G_*<=epsilon*a` gives `G_*<=a*b/(4a+b)`. Since x<1 also gives
`G_*<epsilon*b`, the same guard bound yields `G_*<=b/5`. Thus

```
U(a,b)=min(a/(2+a), a*b/(4*a+b), b/5)
```

is an upper bound for this declared seven-margin/guard/compact family.
Each branch increases with both primitive savings, so exact upper
enclosures for a,b give a valid rational upper bound. This is not a
universal circuit or multiplication lower bound.

Our actual certified b exceeds `4a/(1+a)`, with b>a. The prefix branch
is therefore the minimum. With fixed a,b and this strict ordering, its
supremum is approached by `q->a`, `c->1`, `x->a/b` and
`epsilon->1/(2+a)`. The other margins remain larger; phase scaling and
cell separation have a fixed positive gap. This establishes the scoped
supremum a/(2+a) for the current input ordering, without claiming that
the other two branches are attainable under every extra analytic premise.
